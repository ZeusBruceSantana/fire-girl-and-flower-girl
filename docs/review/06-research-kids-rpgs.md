# Research: what the best kids' RPGs do, and what Ember Bloom should borrow

**Written:** 2026-10-31 · **For:** the owner · **Scope:** games for ages 6 to 10 that are genuinely RPGs
(or teach the way an RPG plays), read against Ember Bloom's design (`docs/GAME-DESIGN.md`, especially
sections 16 and 18).

**How to read this.** Section 1 is one page per game, always in the same five parts: what makes it feel
premium rather than childish, its session loop, how it teaches (or how it would), how it treats failure,
and how it does story and character. Section 2 is the 15 recommendations, ranked, with the game each
one comes from and an effort size (S = a day or two, M = an epic's worth, L = more than one epic).
Section 3 says what I could not verify. Every claim with a source has a link; where I'm going from
general knowledge of the game rather than a source, I say so.

**The one-line verdict.** The games that feel "premium" to a 7-year-old and to the adult next to her share
four things: the battle is a *performance* the player can get stylish at (timed taps, combos, a reacting
crowd), *friendship is a system with levels that change play* (not a counter), *the world remembers you*
(a book that fills in, color that comes back), and *failure is never a wall* (modes you can switch, bosses
you can skip, a hint instead of a buzzer). Ember Bloom already has the bones of all four. The gap is
presentation and depth, not direction.

## 1. Game by game

### 1.1 Prodigy Math (the cautionary tale)

- **What works.** The core hook is sound: a monster blocks the way, a right answer casts the spell. The
  questions adapt to the child's level, each one carries hints, and parents get a dashboard that shows
  what was practiced and what was hard. Common Sense Media calls the adaptive platform "one of the
  highlights" and says it is best for *practice*, not instruction
  ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/prodigy-math-game)).
- **What feels manipulative.** Fairplay (then the Campaign for a Commercial-Free Childhood) counted
  **16 membership ads and 4 math problems in 19 minutes**. Members ride clouds and wear badges while
  non-members "walk in dirt"; members earn more stars and a higher wizard level *for the same work*; a
  "Wizard Watch" shows what friends bought; the sparkly treasure box is an ad for non-members
  ([Fairplay, "7 reasons to say no to Prodigy"](https://fairplayforkids.org/?p=26304)). Twenty-two groups
  took it to the FTC ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2021/02/19/prodigy-math-game-ftc-complaint),
  [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/popular-interactive-math-game-prodigy-is-target-of-complaint-to-federal-trade-commission/2021/02)).
  Common Sense's second concern is subtler and applies to us: the battles, pets and upgrade prompts
  **distract from the math**, and feedback on a wrong answer is thin, so kids learn to click through.
- **Session loop.** Wander, bump a monster, answer one question per spell, loot, shop, repeat. No
  natural end to a session; the game wants you to stay.
- **Teaching.** Questions are standard drill dropped into the fight. Fairplay's sharpest point: the
  math is "not woven into the plot." A Superman-vs-Supergirl survey question has nothing to do with
  the story. That is the "quiz with a cartoon on top" the owner wants to avoid.
- **Failure.** Soft (you can retry), but only one right answer is needed to cast, so guessing works.
- **Story.** Thin, generic wizard-school. The world does not need the child; it needs the subscription.
- **Lesson for us:** keep the one-question-casts-a-spell hook, but (a) make the reward for a *first-try*
  right answer visibly bigger than a retry so guessing is a worse strategy, (b) never let the world
  show the child anything she cannot have, and (c) make the question *about the scene* whenever the data
  allows (the change you give the baker, the word on the crumbs).

### 1.2 Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu! / Eevee! (2018)

- **Premium, not childish.** Everything is readable at a glance: wild Pokémon are *visible on the
  map* (no random ambushes), catching is one physical throw with a shrinking ring, and the partner
  Pokémon rides on your shoulder and reacts to you. Nothing on screen is a baby-fied version of the
  "real" Pokémon; it is simply the real thing with the clutter removed
  ([The Nostalgia Spot, playing it with a young son](https://thenostalgiaspot.com/2019/06/05/pokemon-lets-go-pikachu-or-babys-first-pokemon/),
  [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/pokemon-lets-go-eeveepikachu)).
- **Session loop.** Route → town → gym. Each route is a self-contained 15-minute chunk with new creatures.
- **Co-op for a little sibling.** A second player joins by shaking a controller and gets a second
  battle slot: two actions against one enemy. The reviewer above notes the game *never tells you this
  exists*, and that the support player is dropped in 2-on-2 fights, which is exactly the "absent hero"
  edge case Ember Bloom already handles better.
- **Collection drive.** Catching, not battling, is the main source of experience. Every new creature
  fills a Pokédex page with a silhouette first. The reviewer's son skipped every NPC and got bored in
  dungeons with nothing to catch: for a 7-year-old, *finding* beats *fighting*.
- **Failure.** Fainting sends you to a Pokémon Center; nothing is lost but a little money. Battles are
  "too easy" for adults and that is a deliberate choice.
- **Story.** Minimal and mostly text-only; the reviewer got tired of reading dialogue aloud. Our
  read-aloud rule is the right fix.
- **Lesson for us:** visible roaming troubles (already in v3), a *silhouette collection book* (already
  in), and a second-player "help tap" that needs no menu.

### 1.3 Pokémon Scarlet / Violet (2022)

- **Battle legibility.** From general knowledge (not from a fetched source, see section 3): once you
  have fought a species, the move menu labels each move **"Super effective" / "Not very effective"**
  against it. Children never need the 18-type chart; the game teaches it by showing the label until
  the child notices the pattern. Reviews confirm the type system is the whole tactical layer
  ([GamerGuides type match-ups](https://gamerguides.com/pokemon-scarlet-and-violet/guide/how-to-play/pokemon-battles/type-match-ups)).
- **Onboarding.** Three parallel story paths (gyms, Titans, Team Star), any order. The character
  writing, especially Arven and his sick Pokémon, is what critics praise: "quiet moments between
  fights" rather than world-ending stakes
  ([Inverse](https://inverse.com/gaming/pokemon-scarlet-violet-review),
  [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/pokemon-scarlet-and-violet-review-growth-and-growing-pains-140048933.html)).
- **Accessibility.** The Family Gaming Database lists no quick-reaction requirements and no button
  combos ([Family Gaming Database](https://www.familygamingdatabase.com/accessibility/Pokmon+Scarlet+Pokmon+Violet)).
- **Failure.** Same as Let's Go: faint, return, nothing lost that matters.
- **Lesson for us:** show the elemental hint *on the move button* after the first meeting with a
  trouble, and let the three heroes' three paths (school, woods, town) be done in any order.

### 1.4 Kirby and the Forgotten Land (2022)

- **Premium, not childish.** A full 3D world, cinematic bosses, and a hub town that grows as you rescue
  Waddle Dees. The game is *generous*, not *simplified*.
- **Failure, the model answer.** Two modes, Spring-Breeze (more health) and Wild (more coins), switchable
  at any time inside one save, with no "easy" stamp on completed stages. Nintendo frames the modes as
  "explanations, not judgements." A father plays Wild alone at night and Spring-Breeze with his
  five-year-old by day, in the same save
  ([Vice, "The newest Kirby game doesn't judge how you play"](https://www.vice.com/en/article/the-newest-kirby-game-doesnt-judge-how-you-play)).
  Kirby can flap out of any bad jump, and most enemies can simply be walked past.
- **Session loop.** One stage (8 to 12 minutes) with a visible checklist of secrets, then back to
  Waddle Dee Town where each rescue count unlocks a new shop or minigame.
- **Co-op.** Player 2 is Bandana Waddle Dee: simpler, can't die in a way that matters, and the child
  "sets the pace." Common Sense flags creepy late bosses, age 8+
  ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/kirby-and-the-forgotten-land)).
- **Story.** Almost wordless; told by the town filling up with rescued friends.
- **Lesson for us:** a *parent-set* difficulty that only changes numbers (hint timing, challenge count)
  and never labels the child; a hub (the Lair) that visibly grows with every friend made.

### 1.5 Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door (2004 / 2024 remake)

- **Premium, not childish.** Battles happen *on a stage in front of an audience*. Good timing makes the
  crowd cheer and refill Star Power; a dull turn makes them throw things or walk out. "Stylish moves"
  (an extra tap at the right instant) earn applause and bonus XP. The crowd starts at 50 and grows to
  200 as you level ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_Mario:_The_Thousand-Year_Door),
  [Mario Wiki, Stylish move](https://www.mariowiki.com/Stylish_move)). The remake reviews single out that
  the battle system "hasn't aged a day" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/paper-mario-the-thousand-year-door-review)).
- **Timed hits.** Every attack has an "action command": press at the landing moment for a second hit,
  press when struck to block. Turn-based, but the player is *doing* something every turn
  ([God is a Geek](https://www.godisageek.com/reviews/paper-mario-the-thousand-year-door-review)).
  Important for us: the timing window is wide, and the base attack still lands if you miss it.
- **Partners.** Seven, one active at a time, each with a field skill (flip a switch, cross a gap) and
  its own HP and stylish move. Swapping is instant in the remake.
- **Badges.** Equip badges within a Badge Point budget; each one changes a rule (more power, new
  move, a cosmetic). This is the "build" layer kids and adults both enjoy.
- **Humor.** Rogueport is a seedy port town run by a crime family; Bowser is a running gag of failed
  schemes. Jokes land for the adult; the slapstick lands for the child. Structure: prologue + eight
  chapters, each a self-contained place with its own cast.
- **Failure.** Game over returns to the last save block; the remake adds fast travel and bigger item
  caps to soften grind.
- **Lesson for us:** an audience/crowd reaction in battle, a "stylish tap" bonus, and badges with a
  point budget instead of just a wall of trophies.

### 1.6 Mario & Luigi: Brothership (2024)

- **Premium, not childish.** The best animation in the series ("a meaty feast for the eyes",
  [Inverse](https://inverse.com/gaming/mario-luigi-brothership-review)); dialogue "easy for any young
  reader to grasp" ([Niche Gamer](https://nichegamer.com/reviews/mario-luigi-brothership-review)).
- **Battles.** Two brothers, two buttons; every attack and every dodge is a timed tap; "Bros. Attacks"
  are short choreographed sequences the two perform together. **Battle Plugs** (new) are equippable
  rule-changers that stack and wear out, reviewers' favourite addition
  ([RPGamer](https://rpgamer.com/review/mario-luigi-brothership-review/),
  [RPG Site](https://rpgsite.net/review/16466-mario-luigi-brothership-review)). Criticism: it takes
  ten hours to introduce them, and the pacing drags.
- **Story.** Islands drifting apart; the theme is *reconnecting* people, told through dozens of small
  island casts ("humor and positivity by the boatload",
  [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/rpg/mario-luigi-brothership-review)). Several critics found
  it less witty than older entries; the kid-facing tone was a choice.
- **Failure.** Retry the fight from its start; nothing lost.
- **Lesson for us:** together moves as a *tiny choreography* (each sister taps her beat in order), and
  introduce the build layer (gear effects) in the first hour, not the tenth.

### 1.7 Ni no Kuni: Wrath of the White Witch (2011 / 2019 Switch)

- **Premium, not childish.** Studio Ghibli art and Joe Hisaishi's score; critics call the style
  "childlike" and the visuals "breathtaking" in the same sentence
  ([Gamer Horizon](https://gamerhorizon.com/ni-no-kuni-wrath-of-the-white-witch-review/),
  [Trusted Reviews](https://www.trustedreviews.com/ni-no-kuni-wrath-of-the-white-witch_Games_review)).
  The **Wizard's Companion** is an in-game book (and, on DS, a real 392-page hardback shipped in the
  box and *required* to progress) with spells, a bestiary, lore and stories, meant to be "meandered
  through" ([Engadget, 2010](https://www.engadget.com/2010/09/16/ni-no-kuni-take-a-look-its-in-a-book/),
  [Ni no Kuni wiki](https://ninokuni.fandom.com/wiki/Special_Editions)). Spells are drawn as runes.
- **Teaching (incidental).** Reading the Companion *is* the game's homework, and kids did it because the
  book was beautiful and the spell was on the page.
- **Story.** A boy whose mother dies; a stuffed toy comes alive; "mending the broken-hearted" by moving a
  piece of one person's heart (courage, kindness) to another. Reviewers call the plot conventional but
  emotionally effective. Weak point: a muddled battle system
  ([RPGamer](https://rpgamer.com/review/ni-no-kuni-wrath-of-the-white-witch-switch-review/)).
- **Failure.** Lose a fight → retry or reload; familiars never die permanently.
- **Lesson for us:** the Spellbook and the Myth Scrolls should be *one gorgeous book* the girls want to
  leaf through, with each page unlocking something they can cast.

### 1.8 Yo-kai Watch (2013 / 2015)

- **Premium, not childish.** A fully voiced, high-quality cartoon look, a real town (Springdale) with
  bug-catching, fishing, dungeons and dozens of houses to enter, episodic "one mischief per day"
  structure like a TV show ([Nintendo World Report](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/41420/yo-kai-watch-review),
  [Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/?p=336227)).
- **Everyday mysteries.** Yo-kai are "manifestations of everyday annoyances": the one who makes Dad
  forget his umbrella, the one who makes you say the wrong thing. You *befriend* them by solving the
  problem they cause; the medal they give is "a contract of friendship" and goes in the **Medallium**,
  which is both the collection book and the bench
  ([Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Yo-Kai_Watch_(video_game)),
  [StrategyWiki](https://StrategyWiki.org/wiki/Yo-kai_Watch)). This is the closest existing model to
  Ember Bloom's town cases and "trouble becomes a friend."
- **Session loop.** Morning: pick today's goal with the butler. Explore. Finish the goal, the day ends.
  Daily challenge from the main menu. Night has a (mildly scary) "Terror Time."
- **Failure.** Lose a battle → back at home, keep everything. Terror Time can cost collected items
  (which we would not copy).
- **What frustrates.** Befriending is luck-based; reviewers found that "aggravating"
  ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/yokai-watch-review)). Make friendship *earned by a deed*, never
  rolled.
- **Lesson for us:** cases that are about tiny everyday troubles; every solved trouble becomes a named
  friend with a medal page; a "pick today's goal" moment at the dorm.

### 1.9 Miitopia (2016 / 2021)

- **Premium, not childish.** Casting: the player assigns real people (or anyone) to every role,
  including the villain, so the story is about *their* people. Critics: "ridiculously funny" banter
  ([GameLuster](https://gameluster.com/miitopia-review/),
  [Nintendo World Report](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/57415)).
- **Friendship as a system.** Party members share inn rooms and go on outings; friendship levels unlock
  **assist moves** in battle (a friend blocks a hit for you, two friends combine an attack). Quarrels
  happen (jealousy) and quarrelling pairs stop helping each other until they share a room again
  ([Nintendo, Miitopia friendship tips](https://play.nintendo.com/news-tips/tips-tricks/miitopia-friendship-levels-tips-tricks/)).
  This is the design for "friendship levels change play."
- **Session loop.** Travel a road (auto-walk, pop-up events), a few battles, an inn stop (feed, room,
  outing), repeat. 10 to 15 minutes per inn-to-inn.
- **Failure.** Soft; battles mostly auto-play, the player's job is support and items.
- **Weak point.** The combat is thin; "interest wanes where the memes end." Friendship alone cannot
  carry a game; the fight has to be good too.
- **Lesson for us:** lunch-table friendship levels should unlock *battle assists* from that friend, and
  the sisters themselves should have a friendship level with its own perks.

### 1.10 Cozy Grove (2021) and Animal Crossing: New Horizons (2020)

- **Daily loop as the design.** Cozy Grove runs on the real clock: each day the island is grey, ghost
  bears give two or three tasks, finishing one brings *color flooding back around that bear*, and after
  30 to 60 minutes the campfire says there is nothing more until tomorrow
  ([Nintendo Life](https://nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/cozy_grove),
  [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/123979/cozy-grove-review-bearably-campy)). Critics split:
  some loved the "before-bed ritual," others found the cap frustrating. Each finished task reveals a
  memory fragment of that bear's life: friendship is the story.
- **Animal Crossing.** Villager friendship is a hidden 0 to 255 scale in six bands; a daily greeting,
  a gift and a favour each add points, and higher bands unlock new conversation, nicknames and the
  villager's photo ([Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Friendship)). Nintendo's developers said
  at CEDEC 2020 that they changed villager talk to *always put casual chat first* ahead of
  friendship-gated lines so daily visits stay varied
  ([GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/stories/368235-nintendo-details-their-process-for-creating-unique-villagers-in-a)).
  A soft daily limit stops bingeing and makes the return tomorrow the reward
  ([Pixel Poppers, Nook Miles+ and binge-playing](https://pixelpoppers.com/2020/03/nook-miles-and-binge-playing/)).
- **Failure.** None. Nothing can be failed in either game.
- **Lesson for us:** Ember Bloom's "one school day = one session" is already this. Two things to add:
  a *visible* "the day is complete" moment at bedtime, and friends who say something new each day
  even at level 1.

### 1.11 Wandersong (2018)

- **Premium, not childish.** A hero who cannot fight at all. Singing is the only verb: a colour wheel of
  eight notes, used to talk to animals, calm storms, move platforms, and in the end to sing with a whole
  town ([Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/reviews/review-wandersong),
  [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps4/wandersong)). Each act gives the song a new use.
- **Failure.** The rhythm sections are lenient: reviewers "never failed" one; a late note just sounds
  late. Dancing has no mechanical effect and the designer called it "the most important thing" in the
  game ([Pixel Poppers](https://pixelpoppers.com/review/wandersong/)). Expression without a score.
- **Story.** Starts as a standard "chosen hero" tale, then reveals the Bard is *not* the chosen one and
  the actual Hero is the rival; the ending is widely called one of the most moving in indie games.
- **Lesson for us:** a free-expression verb with no grade (the art easel already is one; a "hum a tune
  to a friend" button would be another), and a story that lets the girls be the clever ones precisely
  because they are *not* the strongest.

### 1.12 Chicory: A Colorful Tale (2021)

- **Premium, not childish.** The world is a colourless colouring book and the brush is your only tool:
  paint to swim through paint, paint a plant to make it explode, paint a shopkeeper's logo because she
  asked, or just paint the whole town because you want to
  ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/review/chicory-a-colorful-tale/chicory-a-colorful-tale-review-color-us-impressed)).
  Only three colours at a time, which keeps every screen looking designed rather than scribbled.
- **Failure.** Bosses can be made unlosable: an accessibility toggle turns off dying, and a separate
  option skips any boss from the pause menu
  ([GamePur, all accessibility options](https://www.gamepur.com/guides/all-accessibility-options-in-chicory-a-colorful-tale),
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicory:_A_Colorful_Tale)).
- **Story.** The janitor picks up the master's brush; the real subject is impostor feelings and a
  mentor who is burned out. The jokes are gentle (everyone is named after food).
- **Lesson for us:** art as a *world verb*, not only a class. Paintings the girls make should show up in
  the world (a shop sign, the Lair wall, a friend's room), and NPCs should *ask* for paintings.

### 1.13 Bug Fables: The Everlasting Sapling (2019)

- Three bugs, Paper Mario rules: timed hits, medals within a budget, and a **party order** that matters
  (front bug takes the hits; some medals change positioning)
  ([RPG Site](https://rpgsite.net/review/9742-bug-fables-the-everlasting-sapling-review),
  [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/53853/bug-fables-switch-review)).
  Reviewers found the *story* ordinary and the *characters* the draw: a brash beetle, a cautious bee,
  a secretive moth, each with their own reason to be there.
- **Lesson for us:** three heroes with three personalities that argue a little is more interesting than
  three heroes who agree; let the dialogue show the Fire/Flower/Electric temperaments.

### 1.14 Slay the Spire (and why there is no "Jr.")

- There is no children's edition; the board game is rated 12+ ([Dice Tower](https://dicetower.com/board-game/338960)).
  What is worth stealing is the *shape*: a visible map with branching nodes (fight, rest, shop, event),
  a small deck you build from drafted cards, and relics that change one rule each. Every decision is one
  tap and the consequence is shown before you commit. (General knowledge; not from a fetched source.)
- **Lesson for us:** show the route as a map of nodes the girls choose between (a chest, a friend, a
  trouble), and let the Spellbook *be* a hand of cards they draft from at level-up.

### 1.15 The Legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom (2024)

- **A princess protagonist.** Zelda does not get a sword by default; she *copies* things (beds, rocks,
  monsters) and summons them as "echoes." Puzzles accept many answers: stack beds into a bridge, send a
  copied monster to fight for you. A limited "Swordfighter form" is the treat, not the baseline
  ([RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/the-legend-of-zelda-echoes-of-wisdom/),
  [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/gaming/the-legend-of-zelda-echoes-of-wisdom-review)).
- **Accessibility.** Puzzles "difficult but not impregnable"; standard mode "challenge without
  frustration." Bark rates it 8+ ([Bark](https://www.bark.us/content-review/legend-of-zelda-echoes-of-wisdom-review/)).
  The developers built it around "being mischievous" ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/?p=37587)).
- **Story.** Deliberately simple, clear good and evil, with worldbuilding texture (the Deku call the
  rifts "dark thorns") and jokes (amateurish wanted posters of Zelda).
- **Lesson for us:** puzzles with several right answers (any spell of the right element works), and
  "collected" troubles the girls can *summon* as helpers.

### 1.16 Reader Rabbit (1986 onward) and Teach Your Monster to Read (2012 onward)

- **Reader Rabbit.** The 1997 SuperKids review: each activity "explains itself in detail and leads the
  child to the correct response, so there is little or no frustration," the commentary feels like "a
  supportive tutor rather than a faceless menu," and words repeat then widen
  ([SuperKids](https://superkids.com/aweb/pages/reviews/reading3/reader1/merge.shtml),
  [retro-replay](https://retro-replay.com/db/dos/reader-rabbit)). Common Sense's complaint about the
  later bundle is the key one: the games "were better when played in a story context, which is missing"
  ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/reader-rabbit-reading-learning-system)).
  Activities that were not quizzes: sort words into a word factory by pattern, match a picture to the
  word that *changes one letter*, build a sentence that a character then acts out.
- **Teach Your Monster to Read.** The child *builds a monster* who then travels three islands (letters
  and sounds → blending → sentences). The monster, not the child, is the learner: the child helps it.
  Mini-games ask you to *do* something with a sound (feed the monster the word that matches, steer to
  the right letter), not pick A/B/C. Prizes dress the monster
  ([Modulo, teacher review](https://www.modulo.app/all-resources/teachyourmonstertoread),
  [Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/app/teach-your-monster-to-read)).
- **Lesson for us:** the reading moment should always be *for* something in the scene (the spell, the
  change, the sign), and at least one challenge type should be "do" rather than "pick": drag letters
  into a word, order the torn note, feed the right word to the trouble.

### 1.17 Khan Academy Kids (2018 onward)

- Free, no ads, no purchases ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/id1378467217)). A bear named Kodi
  greets you, teaches tap and drag, and gives hints when you are stuck; the "Learning Path" is one big
  play button so a child never navigates a menu; rewards are clothes for your character and badges
  ([Khan Kids help](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360006764812),
  [Best Apps for Kids](https://www.bestappsforkids.com/khan-academy-kids)). Content ends around grade 2.
- **Lesson for us:** a single "Play" button that resumes the day, and one character whose job is to
  give hints (we have Madame Cinder, Mr. Abacus, Professor Hoot, and the Constable; one of them should
  be *the* hint-giver in every mode).

### 1.18 DragonBox (Algebra 5+, Numbers)

- **Math as play.** Numbers: "Nooms" are creatures for 1 to 10 that you stack, slice, and let eat each
  other (3 eats 5 and becomes 8). Modes: sandbox against a number line, a ladder you build to a star,
  picture puzzles. No quizzes; the rules of numbers are the physics of the toy
  ([Common Sense Media, Kahoot! Numbers](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/kahoot-numbers-by-dragonbox)).
  Algebra 5+: drag cards onto two trays; the rules of balancing an equation are the rules of the game,
  learned without text ([148Apps](https://www.148apps.com/news/dragonbox-algebra-review),
  [Quartz](https://qz.com/390854/the-video-game-that-teaches-algebra-to-4-year-olds)). Critics: some
  goals are unexplained; Nooms differ only by height; spoken feedback is inconsistent.
- **Lesson for us:** at least one Numbers activity where the *objects* carry the math (coins that
  stack into a 10, potion bottles that combine), so the answer is built rather than typed.

## 2. Fifteen recommendations for Ember Bloom, in priority order

Effort: **S** = a day or two inside an existing epic · **M** = an epic's worth · **L** = more than one
epic or needs new art. "Fits" names the Ember Bloom system it touches.
