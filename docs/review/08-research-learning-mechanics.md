# Research 08: learning mechanics that feel like play

**Date:** 2026-10-31 · **For:** the owner · **Scope:** reading (UFLI-style phonics plus the weekly word list),
early math, repetition and adaptivity, "reading as a verb" in the RPG, and showing progress to a parent.
**Not in scope:** art, code changes, commits. Nothing here is built yet.

Plain-language throughout. Technical terms are explained the first time. Every claim has a link; where the
only source is a vendor's own page, it says so.

---

## 1. The one-line finding

The research and the best games agree on one thing: **learning sticks when the learning act *is* the game
move, not a toll booth in front of it.** Habgood and Ainsworth's "intrinsic integration" study is the
clearest evidence: children who played a maths game where the maths was the sword-fight itself learned
more in fixed time and chose to play it **seven times longer** in free time than children who played the
same game with the maths bolted on between levels
([Habgood & Ainsworth 2011, Journal of the Learning Sciences](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/3556/);
[thesis, University of Nottingham](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/10385)). Prodigy is the cautionary
tale: reviewers call it "chocolate-covered broccoli" because "to cast a spell or open a chest, the game
pauses and forces the player to solve a math problem"
([Screenwise](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/when-educational-games-aren-t-actually-educational);
[Common Dreams on the FTC complaint](https://commondreams.org/news/2021/02/23/complaint-ftc-child-advocates-warn-prodigy-math-game-exploiting-pandemic-prey)).

Ember Bloom today is honest about this: "pick a spell, then power it with a word" is still a toll booth
with a nicer frame. The 15 recommendations below are about moving the reading and the math *into* the
verbs the girls already want to do: cast, unlock, brew, trade, find, talk.

## 2. What the science says practice should look like

### 2.1 Structured literacy and UFLI (reading)

UFLI Foundations is an explicit, systematic phonics program with an eight-step daily routine that is
mostly **review**: phonemic awareness, visual drill (see a letter, say its sound), auditory drill (hear a
sound, write its letter), blending drill, the new concept, word work, irregular "heart words", and finally
reading connected text. Steps 1 to 4 are review; steps 6 to 8 apply the new pattern at word and sentence
level ([UFLI Foundations](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations);
[UFLI pocket reference](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/UFLI-foundations-pocket-reference.pdf);
[scope-and-sequence guide](https://spellingtestbuddy.com/resources/ufli-scope-and-sequence/);
[EdReports review](https://edreports.org/reports/detail/ufli-ela-k-2-2025/first-grade/gateway-one)).

The pieces that matter for a game:

- **Grapheme-phoneme correspondence** (letter → sound, sound → letter) drilled to automatic. UFLI does
  this as a fast chorus; a game can do it as a "which letter makes this sound" tap.
- **Blending**: sounding out c-a-t and sliding it together. This is *the* decoding skill.
- **Phoneme-grapheme mapping** ("sound boxes", also called Elkonin boxes): say the word, count the sounds,
  one box per sound, put the letter(s) for each sound in its box. "sheep" is three boxes: sh / ee / p.
  Reading Rockets gives the procedure and the difficulty ladder (picture + 2 boxes → picture + 3 boxes →
  no picture + 4 boxes) ([Reading Rockets, Elkonin boxes](https://www.readingrockets.org/classroom/classroom-strategies/elkonin-boxes)).
  Ross and Joseph (2018) found evidence that word boxes help preschool-to-elementary children with
  phonemic awareness, letter-sound links and spelling ([summary](https://literacylearn.com/all-about-elkonin-boxes/)).
- **Heart words**: irregular words (said, was, the). UFLI marks the regular parts with blue squares and the
  one irregular part with a red heart; the child learns *only the heart part* by memory, the rest by
  sounding out ([UFLI heart words guide](https://spellingtestbuddy.com/resources/ufli-heart-words/)).
- **Orthographic mapping** is the theory behind all of it (Ehri 2014): a word becomes a "sight word" when
  its spelling, sound and meaning get bonded in memory through repeated *decoding*, not through flash-card
  staring. Needs phonemic awareness and letter-sound knowledge first
  ([Ehri 2014 overview](https://library.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/257/2019/07/ehri.pdf);
  [MA DESE summary](https://www.doe.mass.edu/massliteracy/literacy-block/foundational-skills/orthographic-mapping.docx)).
- **Decodable text**: short passages built from patterns already taught. The evidence is "inconclusive
  but probably beneficial" for children still learning phonics (Cheatham & Allor 2012,
  [Reading Rockets summary](https://www.readingrockets.org/resources/resource-library/influence-decodability-early-reading-text-reading-achievement-review);
  [UNC FPG](https://tri.fpg.unc.edu/decodable-books/)). The known weakness is stilted language, which a
  myth-storybook with funny dialogue can avoid.
- **Retrieval practice** (making the child pull the word out of memory rather than look at it) beats
  copying for first- and second-grade spelling, *as long as corrective feedback follows right away*
  (Jones et al. 2016, [Educational Psychology Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-015-9330-6);
  [2023 replication](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/epub/10.1080/09658211.2023.2248420)). "Build the
  word" is already retrieval practice; "hear it, tap it" is weaker (recognition, not recall).

### 2.4 Repetition and adaptivity for 7-year-olds

- **Spacing works for this age, in short bursts.** A reading study found three 2-minute sessions across the
  day beat one 6-minute session ([Parenting Science summary](https://parentingscience.com/spaced-learning));
  second-graders doing spaced multiplication flashcards beat chanting on accuracy and fluency at a one-week
  delay ([Hendrick, May 2026 dispatch](https://carlhendrick.substack.com/p/the-monthly-dispatch-whats-new-in-3c4)).
  But grade-2 children score lower on retrieval exercises than older children and need more scaffolding
  (Goossens et al., [Learning Scientists](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2017/7/20-1)).
  **Practical rule:** many tiny moments (one word at a chest, one word at a sign) across a 15-minute play
  session beat one five-word "class".
- **A criterion of three** (a word is "learned" when retrieved correctly three times) gave better retention
  at one and four months in a children's word-learning study
  ([Cambridge, 2023](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/CAD724BCD77EB45BE82D698F6B832E6C/S0305000923000752a.pdf/fostering-retention-of-word-learning-the-number-of-training-sessions-children-retrieve-words-positively-relates-to-post-training-retention.pdf)).
  Expanding gaps (short gap first, then longer) help early success for young children; the schedules
  converge later ([Leonard et al. 2024](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2024/2024_Leonard_et_al_JSLHR_Expanding_Retrieval.pdf)).
  Ember Bloom's four Leitner boxes are a sound shape; the fix is *when* words are asked, not the boxes.
- **Interleaving** (mixing word types / puzzle types rather than blocks of one kind) improved an out-of-game
  test in a digital multiplication game ([Ben-David & Roll 2023](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/desirable-difficulties-the-effects-of-spaced-and-interleaved-prac/)),
  but it *feels* harder in the moment and children can read that as "I'm bad at this"
  ([Hendrick](https://carlhendrick.substack.com/p/the-monthly-dispatch-whats-new-in-3c4)). Interleave
  quietly: don't announce "now a hard one".
- **Desirable difficulty** means a little harder than comfortable, not frustrating
  ([Bjork summary](https://dil.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Desirable-difficulties.pdf)). The
  game version is Rigby's "optimal challenge": competence comes from challenge that is just within reach,
  and autonomy from *ownership of what you made*, not just from menus of choices
  (Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006, [paper](https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/the-motivational-pull-of-video-games-a-self-determination-theory-/);
  [PENS](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/player-experience-of-needs-satisfaction-pens/)).
- **Feedback on errors.** Retrieval only helps spellers when corrective feedback follows
  ([Jones 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-015-9330-6)). Confident errors are
  *more* likely to be fixed once corrected (the "hypercorrection effect"), and elementary children
  overestimate how right they were ([Metcalfe lab summary](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-013-0344-3);
  [Southampton replication](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/412186)). For vocabulary, a *delayed* second look
  (next day) beat immediate-only feedback in sixth graders
  ([Metcalfe, Kornell & Finn 2009](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-011-0092-1)). So: fix
  the error now, *and* bring the word back tomorrow.
- **The protégé effect.** Children make more effort when they are teaching a character than when learning
  for themselves; fifth-graders teaching "Betty" out-learned those taught directly
  ([Chase, Chin, Oppezzo & Schwartz 2009](https://purl.stanford.edu/zm369yx3532);
  [Biswas et al. 2008](https://content.iospress.com/articles/international-journal-of-artificial-intelligence-in-education/jai18-3-02)).
  Teach Your Monster is this idea in a costume. Ember Bloom already has companions and troubles-turned-friends;
  it does not yet have anything the girls *teach*.

### 2.5 Showing progress to parents without grades

Every decent parent report found converges on the same four parts: a few plain-word mastery labels, a line
over time, effort shown separately from accuracy, and "what to do next at home".

- Amira's parent report: a progress graph with a dot per activity, strand subscores with a one-line
  explanation of each, and "3 tips for helping" based on the child's own weak spots
  ([Amira one-pager](https://explore.amiralearning.com/hubfs/Parents/Parent-Report-One-Pager.pdf);
  [district guide](https://ga01000549.schoolwires.net/cms/lib/GA01000549/Centricity/Domain/563/AMIRA%20-%20Understanding%20the%20Parent%20Report%20English.pdf)).
- A Dolch-words app shows "Confident / Still learning" plus "the trickiest word and its recent accuracy"
  ([listing](https://mwm.ai/fr/apps/dolch/6762555419)).
- Separate *time spent* from *progress*, since "a child can spend an hour on a phone without learning much"
  ([Kitabu](https://kitabu-ai-web.pages.dev/blog/parent-progress-updates)).
- Keep it low-pressure: "completed stories, skill milestones, and recommended next steps"
  ([Kids Academy](https://www.kidsacademy.mobi/storytime/weekly-progress-reports/)).

Ember Bloom's parent screen already plans "knows it / getting there / tricky". The gaps are the line over
time, the per-pattern view (not just per-word), and the "try this at bedtime" tip.

## 3. Fifteen recommendations, in priority order

Effort: **S** = a day or two inside existing files; **M** = a new data file and a new screen or mechanic;
**L** = a new system touching save, battle and data. All keep the hard rules (no speech recognition,
target word never read first, losing is soft, content in `data/`).

| # | Recommendation | Why (evidence) | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Make the word *be* the spell, not the gate to it.** Each spell in the spellbook has a word on it (from the weekly list, drawn by pattern). Reading the word right *is* the cast: the tap on the right tile fires the effect from the tile itself. Wrong tile → fizzle sparkle and hint, no pause screen. Remove the "pick spell, then challenge pops up" two-step everywhere. | Intrinsic integration: 7× free-time play, better learning ([Habgood](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/3556/)). Prodigy's toll-booth failure ([Screenwise](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/when-educational-games-aren-t-actually-educational)). | M |
| 2 | **Many tiny reading moments across the day, not one five-word class.** Cap any single reading moment at one or two words; spread the Leitner draw across chests, signs, shop labels, NPC lines and battle turns. Class becomes the place the *new pattern* is introduced, in 3 to 4 minutes, with the review happening in the world. | Spaced 2-minute bursts beat one 6-minute block ([Parenting Science](https://parentingscience.com/spaced-learning)); UFLI puts review first and application last ([UFLI](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations)). | M |
| 3 | **Add a pattern layer under the word list.** `data/phonics.js`: a UFLI-ordered list of patterns (short a, digraph sh, magic e, ...). Each parent word is tagged with its pattern automatically (a small rule table) or by the parent. The Leitner picker then spaces *patterns* as well as words, and the game can generate decodable "spare" words from a pattern when the weekly list is thin. | Orthographic mapping needs decoding of the pattern, not word-by-word memory ([Ehri 2014](https://library.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/257/2019/07/ehri.pdf)); UFLI scope and sequence ([guide](https://spellingtestbuddy.com/resources/ufli-scope-and-sequence/)). | M |
| 4 | **Sound boxes as the "build the word" default.** Replace free letter-tile slots with one box per *sound* (sh goes in one box). Tiles say their sound when touched. Hint ladder: picture appears → box count flashes while the helper stretches the word → first box shows faint letter. | Elkonin boxes, with the exact difficulty ladder ([Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org/classroom/classroom-strategies/elkonin-boxes)); tiles that speak are what makes Endless Alphabet work ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/app/endless-alphabet)). | S |
| 5 | **Heart words get a heart.** A parent can mark a word irregular (or the pattern table does it). The game shows the regular letters normally and the irregular part with a small heart; the "build" challenge only asks the child to place the heart letter from memory, the rest is blended. | UFLI heart-word method ([guide](https://spellingtestbuddy.com/resources/ufli-heart-words/); [pocket reference](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/UFLI-foundations-pocket-reference.pdf)). | S |
| 6 | **Add a sound-to-letter tap ("Which letter says /sh/?").** The fastest UFLI drill, and the one Ember Bloom lacks. Three big letter tiles, one sound spoken. Use it as the cheap "spark" cast (level 1 move) so even a tired hero can always act. | UFLI visual/auditory drills ([pocket reference](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/UFLI-foundations-pocket-reference.pdf)); Teach Your Monster's "collect the letter that matches the sound" ([mini-games](https://www.teachyourmonster.org/?p=1377)). | S |
| 7 | **Learned = three clean retrievals, with an expanding schedule.** Box promotion rule: a word moves up only after three first-try successes; first return gap is short (next scene), then a day, then three days. | 8 | **Three-tier help, Lexia-style, per skill not per try.** If a *pattern* has two misses in a row across any moments: next time it appears with two choices instead of three and a picture (Guided). Never announce the tier. | Lexia's Standard → Guided Practice → Direct Instruction branching ([datasheet](https://www.lexialearning.com/user_area/content_media/raw/2023-Q4-CA-C5-Dyslexia-Datasheet-v1-2-WEB.pdf)); interleaving feels hard, so hide the switch ([Hendrick](https://carlhendrick.substack.com/p/the-monthly-dispatch-whats-new-in-3c4)). | M |
| 9 | **Give each girl a creature to *teach*.** The sparkle-friend from a cheered-up trouble becomes a pupil: it "doesn't know" the week's words. The pupil's progress page *is* her progress page in disguise. | Protégé effect: more effort when teaching a character ([Chase et al. 2009](https://purl.stanford.edu/zm369yx3532)); Betty's Brain gains ([Biswas 2008](https://content.iospress.com/articles/international-journal-of-artificial-intelligence-in-education/jai18-3-02)); it is the core of Teach Your Monster. | L |
| 10 | **Give numbers a body.** In Numbers class and the shop, coins and beans are stackable sprites whose height matches their value (a 5-coin is five 1-coins tall). Adding is dragging stacks together; change is slicing a stack. Add a free sandbox corner at the stall with no task. | DragonBox Nooms: proportional size, swallow to add, slice to subtract, plus a sandbox ([DragonBox PDF](https://dragonbox.com/attachment/14/download); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1005050125)). | M |
| 11 | **Run the shop for real: wanting the thing drives the math.** Make-change and coin-count stop being puzzle cards and become the act of buying the ribbon or selling the apples. Customers at the girls' own stall queue with picture orders; the twins split the till (one counts, one gives change) in two-player. | DragonBox Big Numbers' resource loop ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/dragonbox-big-numbers)); Ember Bloom already has shops and a till (section 16.6 of the design). | M |
| 12 | **Stuck detection before hints.** No hint on the first wrong tap; instead, no tap for 8 seconds *or* a second miss triggers rung one. The helper *asks* ("what sound does it start with?") before it *tells*. | Slice Fractions "hints if it senses you're stuck" ([We Are Teachers](https://www.weareteachers.com/slice-fractions-math-game/)); Kodi asks questions and gives hints ([KA Kids](https://sites.google.com/khanacademy.org/khanacademykids/getting-students-into-the-app/three-ways-to-play)). | S |
| 13 | **Make every word a recall, not a recognition, at least once per box.** Word-to-picture and hear-and-pick are recognition; promote a word to box 3 only after a *build* (recall) success. Keep recognition for the cheap moments. | Retrieval beats copying for grade 1–2 spelling with immediate feedback ([Jones 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-015-9330-6)). | S |
| 14 | **Decodable story pages in the Myths book, generated from the patterns learned.** Between myth pages, a one-line "the owl **ran** to the **hat**" page in the twin's own patterns, with one tappable word she must read (word-to-picture) to turn the page. Keep the voice funny so it doesn't read as a primer. | Decodable text helps while phonics is still being learned ([Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org/resources/resource-library/influence-decodability-early-reading-text-reading-achievement-review)); Duolingo ABC's lesson-then-story rhythm ([SmartBrief](https://www.smartbrief.com/original/strengthen-early-literacy-skills-with-duolingo-abc)). | M |
| 15 | **A parent page in four strips, no grades.** (a) Confident / Getting there / Tricky, by *pattern* and by word, with the trickiest word's last three tries; (b) a small line of first-try rate per week; (c) minutes played shown separately from words learned; (d) one "try this at bedtime" tip picked from the weakest pattern (a pre-written tip per pattern in `data/phonics.js`). Export as a text file. | Amira's graph + strand explanations + 3 tips ([one-pager](https://explore.amiralearning.com/hubfs/Parents/Parent-Report-One-Pager.pdf)); effort vs progress split ([Kitabu](https://kitabu-ai-web.pages.dev/blog/parent-progress-updates)); low-pressure framing ([Kids Academy](https://www.kidsacademy.mobi/storytime/weekly-progress-reports/)). | M |

Suggested order: 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 12, 13 first (mostly S, and they change the feel most); then 3, 5, 8, 15
(the pattern layer and the parent view belong together); then 10, 11, 14; then 9 last because it is the
biggest and the most fun.

One thing *not* to do: no timers, no words-per-minute, even though fluency research uses them. Timed
repeated reading is "less authentic" and only for children who need extra push
([Reach All Readers](https://reachallreaders.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/transcript-75.pdf)), and it
breaks hard rule 5. Measure fluency as "first-try rate over time" instead.

## 4. Six new verbs: reading and math as things you *do*

Each is a mechanic, not a quiz type. All are tap-based, two-player-friendly, and end in success. Data for
each lives in `data/`, never in `js/`.

### Verb 1: Pick the lock (sound boxes as a lock)
A chest or door has a row of 2 to 4 tumblers, one per *sound* in the key word. The helper stretches the
word once ("ssshhh-eee-p"). The girl drops letter tiles into tumblers; a right tile clicks and the tumbler
turns, a wrong one spits out with a boing. All tumblers turned → the lock springs. Two players: each has
her own lock on her side of the chest; both open → the shared loot is doubled. *Teaches:* phoneme-grapheme
mapping, blending. *Source pattern:* Elkonin boxes ([Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org/classroom/classroom-strategies/elkonin-boxes)).

### Verb 2: Cast by the tile (the word is the wand)
Spells in the spellbook are shown as a word on a card. Three cards fan out, one is the spell she chose
(say, "Glow"); the other two are decoys from her list. Tapping the right card fires *from the card*: it
flies at the trouble and bursts. Tapping a decoy: the decoy wobbles, the helper says its first sound, the
cards stay. There is no separate challenge screen; the battle never pauses. Elemental matching stays as
is. *Teaches:* decoding under mild excitement, sight recognition of own words.
*Source pattern:* intrinsic integration ([Habgood](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/3556/)); Typing of the Dead's
"the word is the bullet" ([MobyGames](https://www.mobygames.com/game/3268/)).

### Verb 3: Brew the potion (recipe as a sentence)
A cauldron and a shelf of labelled jars (labels are words from her list plus pattern-generated decoys).
The recipe scroll shows a two- or three-word line in pictures-plus-words: "**red** **bean**, **hot**
**sand**". She reads a label and drags that jar in. Right jar → a colour change and a pop; wrong jar →
a silly fizz and the jar hops back. Finished potion is a real crafted item. Two players: one reads the
recipe aloud to the other (a "read it to a sister" version of read-to-a-grown-up). *Teaches:* word-to-
meaning, short connected text, heart words in context.
*Source pattern:* Scribblenauts' "write the word, get the object" ([Nintendo page](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-DS/Scribblenauts-272827.html)); Endless Alphabet's meaning vignette.

### Verb 4: Read the sign to find the secret
Maps have signposts and shop boards with a word on them (silent, never spoken first). Tapping a sign is
word-to-picture: three small picture bubbles pop up; the right one opens a hidden path, a sparkle or a
friend's house. Signs reuse the week's words so the map itself becomes the review. A sign she has read
three times becomes "known": it shows its picture permanently. *Teaches:* decoding with a purpose,
cumulative review disguised as exploration. *Source pattern:* UFLI's "connected text" step; Teach Your
Monster's exploration islands.

### Verb 5: Run the till (make change with stacks that have size)
At the girls' own stall, a customer holds up a picture order and a stack of coins. Coins are stacks whose
height equals value. She drags goods across and *slices* the customer's stack to take the price, handing
the rest back; or, when selling, stacks coins into the customer's hand until the stacks match. A wrong
amount: the customer scratches its head and the two stacks sit side by side so the difference is visible,
then she adjusts. Two players: one counts the order, one makes change; the till shows both. *Teaches:*
counting, adding and subtracting to 20, change-making, more/less by eye.
*Source pattern:* DragonBox Nooms and Big Numbers ([DragonBox](https://DragonBox.com/products/big-numbers); [Common Sense](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/dragonbox-big-numbers)).

### Verb 6: Teach your sparkle
A tamed trouble (a sparkle) follows each girl and "wants to learn". Each day it asks for one word from her
list; she reads it (any of the verbs above). Later that day, at a sign or a lock, the sparkle *has a go
first* and gets it right or wrong according to her own recent history with that word. If it gets it
wrong, she "helps it" by doing the word again (a hidden second retrieval). The sparkle's badge wall fills
as she masters patterns; the parent page is the same data. NPC dialogue also comes through the sparkle:
townsfolk lines have one tappable word choice that changes the reply ("Do you want the **red** one or the
**bed** one?"), so reading steers the conversation. *Teaches:* retrieval, self-monitoring, motivation.
*Source pattern:* protégé effect ([Chase 2009](https://purl.stanford.edu/zm369yx3532)); Harvard's
"Read to Me!" where players read to pet dragons ([Harvard GSE](https://studio.gse.harvard.edu/read-to-me)).

## 5. Things the research does *not* settle

- Nobody has published a good study of session length for app-based reading at age 7. The 2-minute and
  6-minute figures come from classroom work. Watch the twins and let the "class length" setting decide.
- Decodable-text evidence is thin and the texts can be stilted. Keep the myths as proper stories and use
  decodable lines only as short bridges.
- Most app claims (Reading Eggs, Duolingo ABC, Lexia's branching, Teach Your Monster's new version) are
  the vendors' own descriptions. The design patterns are still worth copying; the effect sizes are not
  worth quoting.
- The PENS "competence" scale may not be separate from "intuitive controls"
  ([Johnson, Gardner & Perry 2018](https://game-evaluation.github.io/questionnaires/repository/pens.html)),
  which for a 7-year-old is a reminder that *big, obvious tap targets* are part of feeling capable.
